# Contract Playbook: Non-Disclosure Agreements

This playbook sets the company's positions for non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) with vendors, partners, and customers. For each clause, it gives the standard position, the fallback, and the red flags. The standard position is what the company signs. A fallback is weaker than the standard position: the business may accept it without legal review, but must first ask the other party for the standard position. A red flag must go to the legal team.

## Mutuality

- **Standard position:** The NDA is mutual, so both parties protect each other's confidential information on the same terms.
- **Fallback:** A one-way NDA in which only the other party discloses information to the company.
- **Red flag:** A one-way NDA in which only the company discloses information, or in which the company's obligations are stricter than the other party's.

## Definition of confidential information

- **Standard position:** Confidential information is information that is marked as confidential, or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential from its nature and the circumstances of disclosure.
- **Fallback:** Only information that is marked as confidential, or identified as confidential in writing within 30 days after an oral disclosure.
- **Red flag:** A definition that covers all information exchanged, whether or not it is confidential, or that includes information the company develops independently.

## Exclusions

- **Standard position:** Information is not confidential if it is public through no fault of the recipient, was known to the recipient before disclosure, is received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality, or is independently developed by the recipient.
- **Fallback:** The same exclusions, with the recipient bearing the burden of proving that an exclusion applies.
- **Red flag:** Missing exclusions for independently developed information or for information that is already known to the recipient.

## Term and confidentiality period

- **Standard position:** The agreement lasts 2 years, and the duty to keep information confidential lasts 3 years after the information is disclosed.
- **Fallback:** A confidentiality period of up to 5 years. Trade secrets may stay protected for as long as they remain trade secrets.
- **Red flag:** A perpetual or indefinite confidentiality period for all information, or a confidentiality period longer than 5 years for information that is not a trade secret.

## Permitted disclosures

- **Standard position:** The recipient may share confidential information with its employees, contractors, and professional advisers who need to know it and who are bound by confidentiality duties at least as strict as the NDA.
- **Fallback:** Disclosure to affiliates, under the same conditions.
- **Red flag:** No right to share information with advisers or contractors, or a requirement to get written consent before every internal disclosure.

## Compelled disclosure

- **Standard position:** The recipient may disclose information when the law or a court requires it, after notifying the discloser promptly where the law allows, so that the discloser can seek a protective order.
- **Fallback:** Reasonable efforts to notify the discloser, instead of an absolute duty to notify.
- **Red flag:** No exception for disclosures that the law requires.

## Return or destruction of information

- **Standard position:** On request, the recipient returns or destroys confidential information within 30 days. It may keep copies in automatic backups and copies that the law requires it to keep, and these stay confidential.
- **Fallback:** Certification of destruction by an officer of the recipient, if the other party asks for it.
- **Red flag:** A duty to destroy all copies including backups, or to return information within less than 10 days.

## Ownership and licenses

- **Standard position:** Each party keeps ownership of its own confidential information. The NDA grants no license or other rights, except the right to use the information for the stated purpose.
- **Fallback:** None. The standard position is required.
- **Red flag:** Any clause that assigns or licenses the company's intellectual property, or that gives the other party ownership of feedback, ideas, or work product.

## Residuals

- **Standard position:** No residuals clause.
- **Fallback:** A residuals clause that covers only general skills and know-how retained in unaided memory, and that excludes the discloser's patents, copyrights, and trade secrets.
- **Red flag:** A residuals clause that lets the other party use any information that its staff remember, without exclusions.

## Liability and remedies

- **Standard position:** Each party is liable for direct damages caused by its breach. Both parties may seek injunctive relief for a breach or threatened breach.
- **Fallback:** A liability cap of no less than USD 1,000,000 for breach of confidentiality.
- **Red flag:** Uncapped liability for the company only, liquidated damages or penalties, or a waiver of the company's right to injunctive relief.

## Indemnity

- **Standard position:** No indemnity in an NDA.
- **Fallback:** A mutual indemnity limited to third-party claims caused by a breach of the NDA.
- **Red flag:** A one-way indemnity from the company, or an indemnity that covers the other party's own negligence.

## Non-solicitation

- **Standard position:** No non-solicitation clause in an NDA.
- **Fallback:** A mutual non-solicitation of employees who worked directly on the project, for up to 12 months, that does not restrict general job advertisements.
- **Red flag:** A non-solicitation period longer than 12 months, a one-way restriction on the company, or a restriction that covers all employees.

## Non-compete

- **Standard position:** No non-compete clause.
- **Fallback:** None. Any non-compete clause must go to the legal team.
- **Red flag:** Any restriction on the company developing, buying, or selling competing products or services.

## Governing law and jurisdiction

- **Standard position:** The laws of the State of New York govern the agreement, and disputes are heard in the courts of New York County.
- **Fallback:** The laws of England and Wales or of Singapore, with the courts of the same place.
- **Red flag:** Any other governing law, mandatory arbitration, or exclusive jurisdiction in the other party's home courts.

## Assignment

- **Standard position:** Neither party may assign the agreement without the other's written consent, except to a successor in a merger or sale of substantially all of its assets.
- **Fallback:** Assignment to an affiliate, with notice to the other party.
- **Red flag:** A right for the other party to assign the agreement freely, including to a competitor of the company.

## Publicity

- **Standard position:** Neither party may announce the relationship or use the other's name or logo without written consent.
- **Fallback:** None. The standard position is required.
- **Red flag:** A right for the other party to name the company as a customer or partner without consent.
